About

A project of Elton University

Gross Domestic Suffering is an open research project of Elton University, a nonprofit university, building a better alternative to GDP: a measure of the preventable suffering we can work together to end.

Our mission

We believe the success of a nation should be judged by how much avoidable suffering its people and animals endure, not only by how much it produces. Our mission is to measure that suffering openly and rigorously, so citizens, researchers and governments can see it, compare it and reduce it.

Based at Elton University

Elton University is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit university based in the United States, with students studying online from anywhere or in person in San Francisco. Its mission is "to provide the highest possible quality education for everyone." Educator and researcher Adam Braus founded Elton in 2021 to build a university on what the evidence says about how people learn, with personalized degrees taught one-on-one by expert professors.

Gross Domestic Suffering fits Elton's commitment to serving students, the country and the world. The project connects to Elton's master's program in Heterodox Economics, where students question conventional economic measures and build better ones.

Created by Adam Braus

Gross Domestic Suffering was created by Adam Braus, an educator, writer and the founder of Elton University. It began with a simple question: if GDP measures everything except what makes life worthwhile, what would it look like to measure the opposite?

Leading economists have asked the same question. In September 2020, Nobel laureate Paul Krugman warned in The New York Times that "despite job gains we're in the midst of a huge increase in national misery." Gross Domestic Suffering measures that misery directly.

The answer is an index of preventable suffering across illness, gender and sexual discrimination, violence, unfreedom, deprivation and animal suffering. It scores 188 countries for every year since 2000. The Netherlands, Sweden and Denmark suffer least. The United States, the world's largest economy, ranks 61st, behind China, Albania, Kazakhstan and Türkiye.

The book

The Future of Good

How to Tell Good from Evil in an Age of Distraction, Polarization, and Crisis

In The Future of Good (Peripatetic Press, 2022), Adam Braus revives a century-old theory of good and evil and applies it to today's moral challenges, drawing on history, philosophy, psychology and neuro- and evolutionary biology. The book shows how to build good institutions, recognize good leaders and live as good people. Gross Domestic Suffering asks the same kind of question of whole countries: how much preventable suffering do they allow?

Our principles

  • Measure outcomes. We count suffering that happens, such as deaths, disease, violence and imprisonment, rather than policies or intentions.
  • Focus on what's preventable. Every indicator tracks harm that some countries have already reduced, which shows it can be reduced.
  • Work in the open. Our methods, data and code are public, and we welcome criticism.
  • Include all who suffer. We measure human-caused animal suffering alongside human suffering.
